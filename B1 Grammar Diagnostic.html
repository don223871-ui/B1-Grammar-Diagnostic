B1 Grammar Diagnostic

Part 1 — Grammar & Language Control

1. I ___ English for about two years.

✅ Correct answer: B — have studied

Why? We use the Present Perfect with for + a period of time when an activity started in the past and continues up to the present.

A — study ❌ Simple Present describes habits or general facts. It does not normally express an activity continuing from the past.

B — have studied ✅ Present Perfect connects the past with the present.

C — am studying ❌ Present Continuous focuses on something happening around now, rather than the duration from the past.

D — studied ❌ Simple Past normally refers to a finished past period.

Key point: have/has + past participle is the basic Present Perfect structure.

2. She ___ to the gym three times this week.

✅ Correct answer: C — has been

Why? This week is an unfinished time period. Present Perfect is appropriate when something has happened during a period that is still continuing.

A — goes ❌ Simple Present usually describes routines or habits, not the number of completed visits during this week.

B — went ❌ Simple Past is more natural with a finished time period such as last week.

C — has been ✅ This means she has visited the gym three times so far this week.

D — is going ❌ Present Continuous normally describes something happening now or a temporary arrangement.

Key point: Expressions such as this week, today, this month often work with Present Perfect.

3. When I arrived home, my brother ___ dinner.

✅ Correct answer: A — was cooking

Why? The cooking was already in progress when another past event happened: I arrived home.

A — was cooking ✅ Past Continuous shows an action that was in progress at a particular moment in the past.

B — cooks ❌ Simple Present does not fit this past-time context.

C — has cooked ❌ Present Perfect does not normally fit a sentence containing a specific past event.

D — will cook ❌ Future meaning does not fit the past context.

Key point: A common pattern is: When + Simple Past + Past Continuous.

4. If I have enough money, I ___ a new laptop.

✅ Correct answer: B — will buy

Why? This is the First Conditional, used for a real or possible future situation.

A — bought ❌ Simple Past does not form the normal First Conditional pattern here.

B — will buy ✅ The structure is: If + Present Simple, will + base verb.

C — would buy ❌ Would is normally associated with the Second Conditional for hypothetical situations.

D — have bought ❌ Present Perfect does not express the expected future result here.

5. You ___ wear a uniform at this school. It's optional.

✅ Correct answer: C — don't have to

Why? Don't have to means there is no obligation. The sentence says wearing a uniform is optional.

A — mustn't ❌ Means something is prohibited.

B — shouldn't ❌ Means something is not advisable or recommended.

C — don't have to ✅ Means something is not necessary.

D — can't ❌ Usually expresses inability or lack of permission.

Important distinction: mustn't = prohibited vs. don't have to = not necessary.

6. This book ___ by millions of people every year.

✅ Correct answer: A — is read

Why? The book receives the action: people read the book. Therefore we need the Passive Voice.

A — is read ✅ Present Simple Passive = is + past participle.

B — reads ❌ This would make the book the performer of the action.

C — was reading ❌ Past Continuous does not fit the general statement every year.

D — has read ❌ This is Active Voice and would make the book the reader.

7. I enjoy ___ English with my friends.

✅ Correct answer: D — speaking

Why? The verb enjoy is followed by a gerund (-ing form).

A — speak ❌ The base form cannot directly follow enjoy.

B — to speak ❌ Enjoy is not normally followed by to + verb.

C — spoke ❌ Simple Past cannot function as the complement of enjoy.

D — speaking ✅ enjoy + verb-ing is the standard structure.

Useful pattern: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind + verb-ing

8. I've never ___ such an interesting film before.

✅ Correct answer: B — seen

Why? The sentence uses Present Perfect: have/has + past participle. The past participle of see is seen.

A — see ❌ Base form cannot follow I've in Present Perfect.

B — seen ✅ I've never seen... is grammatically correct.

C — saw ❌ Saw is Simple Past, not the past participle.

D — seeing ❌ The -ing form cannot follow I've in this structure.

9. My teacher asked me ___ I understood the question.

✅ Correct answer: C — if

Why? The original question is essentially: "Do you understand the question?" This is a Yes/No question. Therefore an indirect question can use if or whether.

A — what ❌ What asks for information about a thing or action.

B — why ❌ Why asks for a reason.

C — if ✅ Correctly introduces a Yes/No indirect question.

D — which ❌ Which normally requires a choice between specific alternatives.

10. I wish I ___ more free time.

✅ Correct answer: A — had

Why? Wish + Past Simple is used to talk about a present situation that is different from what we want.

A — had ✅ I wish I had more free time means I don't have enough free time now and I want the situation to be different.

B — have ❌ Present Simple is not the normal form after wish for an unreal present situation.

C — will have ❌ This does not express the standard unreal-present meaning.

D — would have ❌ Wish + would is generally used for complaints or desired changes, rather than simply describing something we want to possess now.

Key point: I wish I had... = I don't have it now, but I want the present situation to be different.
